These images are the second set of Carnegie-sauropod stiches created for us by Jarrod Davis <jarrod@jarroddavis.com>. He describes the process in the email thread "Panorama" of 2 May 2022.

---- begin quote ----
Hey Mike! So I used Microsoft’s Image Composite Editor, which is pretty amazing but sadly discontinued (but plot twist: still available because The Internet…) to do this. I did three different projection types, they all vary a bit in how the warps happen at the edges. I tried to center along the spine for reference, to make that the straightest, least-warped part of the image. As for the edges, it’s hard to know what’s more true to life, or more useful/desired. So there are three options in here, pick any or all, or let me know if there’s a part that’s important to be warped less, or differently.

I also left the edges raw and uncropped so you can decide how much to keep yourself. Simply trimming to the minimums usually ends up leaving good subject matter out.

Let me know what you think!

Jarrod
---- end quote ----
